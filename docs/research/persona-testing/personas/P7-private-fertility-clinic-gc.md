# P7 · Genetic counsellor in a private IVF and fertility clinic

## Setting
- Genetic counsellor at a private fertility group with several clinics [A].
- Carrier screening results, preimplantation testing, donor conception,
  recurrent pregnancy loss [E6] [A].
- About 15 consults a week, half telehealth [A].

## Devices and IT limits
- Clinic-issued laptop, own login; the group's IT team is small and pragmatic,
  but has a privacy officer who signs off on tools that touch patient data [E22] [A].
- Clinic software is a fertility-specific record system that accepts PDF
  attachments [E25] [A].

## Current tools and workflow
- Draws on paper or in a generic diagram tool; pregnancy losses, donors and
  partners from previous relationships are the complicated bits [A].
- Puts a pedigree PDF in the patient's record and the referral to the obstetrician [E25] [E27].

## Frustrations
1. Pregnancy loss, donor gametes, surrogacy and half-siblings are hard to draw
   correctly and quickly [A].
2. Couples' two families need to be drawn side by side, with consanguinity checks [A].
3. Output needs to look professional for referring specialists [E27].

## Goals and what "better" means to them
- Correct symbols for pregnancies, losses, donors, and both partners' families.
- Clean branded PDF.

## Attitude to new tools and money
- The clinic could pay a modest per-seat fee; the privacy officer is the gate [E22] [E29] [A].

## Standards literacy
- High on reproductive notation (SAB, TOP, ECT, donor, surrogate); expects
  stillbirth not to be drawn as a triangle.

## Usability behaviour
- Confident; uses keyboard and mouse [A].
- Laptop 1440 x 900, Chrome [A].
- Patience budget: 15 minutes [A].

## What this persona cannot tell us
- How Australian fertility clinics handle donor privacy on pedigrees [E9].
- Whether the privacy officer would approve the tool.
