# P1 · Private cancer genetic counsellor, mostly telehealth

## Setting
- Certified genetic counsellor, about 10 years' experience, the last 3 in a small
  private familial-cancer practice (two counsellors and a part-time admin) [A].
- Cancer genetics, the largest specialty [E2]. Private practice is a small share
  of the workforce [E4], but owns its own tooling decisions [E22].
- Most consults are video calls with patients referred by breast surgeons and GPs
  across a whole state; some in person one day a week [E3] [A].
- About 12 new families and 8 follow-ups a week [A].

## Devices and IT limits
- Own MacBook, logged in as herself, Chrome; nobody else uses it [E22] [A].
- A second screen: video call on one, notes on the other [A].
- Practice management system is cloud-based (appointments, letters, billing); it
  stores PDF attachments but has no family-history structure [E25] [A].
- No IT department. The practice owner (her colleague) approves tools over coffee [E29].

## Current tools and workflow
- Draws a rough pedigree on paper during the call, then redraws it in the free
  Progeny online tool after the call for the letter [E10] [E13] [A].
- Types the family again into CanRisk for breast/ovarian risk [E23] [E24].
- Attaches a PDF of the pedigree to the letter to the referrer [E25] [E27].
- So the family is entered three times: paper, Progeny, CanRisk [E23].

## Frustrations
1. Re-entering the same family into CanRisk after drawing it [E23].
2. Redrawing paper into software after the call: 15 to 20 minutes per family [A].
3. The free Progeny tool's PDF looks generic; she wants her practice's letterhead [E27] [A].
4. Can't share her screen to confirm the family with the patient while drawing on paper [E3] [A].

## Goals and what "better" means to them
- Draw once, live on the call, with the patient confirming on a shared screen.
- One file that feeds CanRisk and produces a referral-ready PDF.

## Attitude to new tools and money
- Actively looking; in the 41.8% considering a change [E12].
- Would pay a small monthly subscription out of the practice budget if it saves
  time per family; would not pay for an enterprise licence [E29] [A].
- Will try a tool the same day a colleague recommends it [E29].

## Standards literacy
- High. Uses NSGC 2022 symbols day to day. Would immediately notice a wrong
  deceased slash, missing proband arrow, or twins drawn from separate points when
  zygosity is unknown.

## Usability behaviour
- Comfortable with software; uses keyboard shortcuts once she learns them [A].
- Laptop screen, 1440 x 900 [A].
- Patience budget: 15 minutes for a first try; she will skim help but not read a manual [A].
- Mentally compares every step with Progeny's free tool.

## What this persona cannot tell us
- Australian private practice numbers and pricing norms [E9].
- Whether CanRisk export would actually be used versus typing directly into CanRisk.
- Real per-family timings.
