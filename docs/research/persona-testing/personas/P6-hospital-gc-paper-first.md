# P6 · Hospital genetic counsellor who draws on paper

## Setting
- Genetic counsellor, 5 years' experience, in a regional public hospital's
  outreach genetics clinic; general genetics, cardiac and some cancer [A].
- Travels to satellite clinics; many consults by telehealth from a hospital room [E3] [A].
- Part of the half of counsellors who still draw by hand [E10].

## Devices and IT limits
- Hospital-issued laptop plus shared clinic PCs; logs in as himself, but can't
  install software [E18] [A].
- No pedigree software licence in his service [A].

## Current tools and workflow
- Draws on paper in clinic; scans into the EHR as a PDF [E16] [E25].
- Rewrites relevant relatives in the clinic letter and retypes into CanRisk for
  cancer families [E23] [E24].
- Keeps a filing cabinet of paper pedigrees going back years [E31] [A].

## Frustrations
1. Hand-drawn scans are hard to read and impossible to update; every follow-up
   means redrawing [E16] [A].
2. On telehealth he can't show the patient the drawing to check it [E3] [A].
3. He feels behind colleagues who use software [A].

## Goals and what "better" means to them
- Draw as fast as on paper, with a cleaner result that is easy to update.
- Get his old paper pedigrees into a digital form one day [E31].

## Attitude to new tools and money
- Open, but won't risk a privacy breach; would ask his manager first [E18] [A].
- Hasn't tried to get software approved; assumes it would be refused [A].

## Standards literacy
- High on paper; uses his own shorthand for some annotations [A].

## Usability behaviour
- Moderate tech comfort; mouse-first; compares everything with the speed of a pen [A].
- Laptop, 1366 x 768, Edge [A].
- Patience budget: 10 minutes [A].

## What this persona cannot tell us
- Whether his service would allow a photo of a paper pedigree to leave the device [E31].
- Real paper-versus-software timings (H1).
