# P4 · Course convenor, Master of Genetic Counselling

## Setting
- Genetic counsellor and academic; convenes the pedigree and genetic-risk unit of
  a master's programme, about 20 students a cohort [A].
- Still does one clinic a fortnight to stay current [A].

## Devices and IT limits
- University-managed laptop; can use any website, but installing software needs
  IT [A]. Teaching is in PowerPoint and the university learning platform [E8] [A].
- Recommending a tool to students is her call, but she worries about student
  privacy and about tools disappearing mid-semester [E22] [A].

## Current tools and workflow
- Draws teaching pedigrees in PowerPoint shapes or the free Progeny tool, then
  screenshots [E8] [E13] [A].
- Sets pedigree-drawing exercises from case vignettes and marks them by hand [E5] [A].

## Frustrations
1. Making clean, consistent teaching figures takes hours [E8] [A].
2. Marking student pedigrees for symbol errors is tedious [A].
3. Tools change or vanish; she needs something stable for a whole year [A].

## Goals and what "better" means to them
- Crisp vector export (SVG/PDF) for slides and exam papers [E8].
- A tool she can tell 20 students to use without accounts or cost [E29].
- Ideally, a way to share a case file students open and complete.

## Attitude to new tools and money
- Will adopt a free tool that is clearly standards-correct; a single endorsement
  from her reaches the whole cohort every year [E29] [A].
- Department might pay a small licence if it supports teaching [A].

## Standards literacy
- Expert. Teaches NSGC 2022 and would spot any non-standard symbol. Will judge the
  whole tool on one wrong symbol.

## Usability behaviour
- Methodical; tries every symbol variant, looks for a legend and export options [A].
- Laptop plus projector; checks how it looks at 1280 x 720 [A].
- Patience budget: 30 minutes, because she's evaluating for a cohort [A].
